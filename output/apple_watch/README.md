## What's better than the reference
- Real 3D watch shots (front with a live heart-rate screen, back with the sensor dome, 2 green LEDs + 4 photodiodes, the watch on a holographic wrist), not flat side-views only.
- The cross-section shows the full mechanism: LED → light zigzags into the tissue → some is absorbed by the blood, the rest returns to the photodiodes; the vessel swells with each beat and a "light back" meter drops at the same moment.
- "Why green, not red or blue?" is answered visually: three colours, three fates (red goes deep, blue stops in the top layer, green is absorbed by the blood).
- The counting step is spelled out: peaks numbered 1, 2, 3… then a 60-second bar, then **72 beats per minute**.
- Heartbeat SFX synced to the on-screen pulse, footsteps on the run, monitor beeps on each waveform peak.

## Colour rule
Green appears **only** as the LED light (it is the real colour of the light and the subject of the script). Blood is a muted wine red, because it is the real colour of blood. Everything else is KROZO navy, blue and cyan; the heart icon on the watch is brand blue.

## Fact-check (all claims in the narration are correct)
| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Green LEDs + light-sensitive photodiodes measure blood flow at the wrist (photoplethysmography) | ✅ | [Apple Support — Monitor your heart rate](https://support.apple.com/en-AU/120277) |
| Blood absorbs green light; more blood during a beat → more green absorbed | ✅ | [Apple Support (HT204666)](https://support.apple.com/en-my/HT204666) |
| The LEDs flash very fast ("hundreds of times per second" — on-screen chip) | ✅ Apple's wording | same page |
| The watch counts repeating changes to get beats per minute | ✅ | same page |

**Simplifications (flagged on screen as illustration):** the cross-section is magnified and stylised, vessel swelling is exaggerated ("التمدد مبالغ فيه للتوضيح"), and the red/blue comparison is simplified (blue mostly doesn't reach the vessels; red/infrared go deeper and are absorbed less, so green gives the clearest beat-to-beat signal). Apple also uses infrared light for some background readings; the script doesn't mention it, which is fine.
No Apple logos are shown; the watch is a generic model.
